# 15 - Research-To-Demo Explanation Guide

## Purpose

This note connects the research fields from `FYP_RESEARCH.docx` to the MyPay implementation and explains what should be said during the live demo.

The key presentation idea is:

"The demo should not only show screens. It should show how each screen answers a research gap identified in the literature review."

## Research Fields Found In FYP_RESEARCH

The document covers these main fields:

- FinTech and e-wallet evolution.
- Super-app limitations and the breadth-over-depth problem.
- Collaborative financial management, also described as social finance.
- Psychological barriers in shared expense recovery.
- Fair division and expense splitting theory.
- Numerical precision and allocation of rounding remainders.
- Transaction netting and settlement efficiency.
- Multi-currency exchange mechanics.
- Distributed microservice architecture.
- Service communication patterns.
- E-wallet security controls.
- Similar systems: Splitwise, Touch 'n Go eWallet, and Revolut.
- Technical stack: Spring Boot, Spring Cloud Gateway, Eureka, Config Server, MySQL, Redis, RabbitMQ, OpenFeign, React, Vite, and Docker Compose.

## How The Research Relates To MyPay

### 1. FinTech And E-Wallet Evolution

Research point:

Digital payments evolved from cash, cards, internet banking, and mobile-first e-wallet ecosystems. However, many current e-wallets focus mainly on individual, P2P, and B2C payments.

MyPay relation:

MyPay extends the e-wallet idea beyond simple payment transfer. It supports wallet balances, but also adds shared collections, expense shares, settlement, reports, and notifications.

Live demo explanation:

"I start from the wallet dashboard because MyPay is still an e-wallet. However, unlike a basic wallet, the dashboard also shows social financial positions such as what I owe, what others owe me, and my net position."

### 2. Breadth-Over-Depth Problem In Super-Apps

Research point:

Super-apps provide many features, but their expense-splitting functions are often shallow because they optimize for common merchant or P2P use cases.

MyPay relation:

MyPay focuses deeply on one specialized area: collaborative expense management with integrated settlement.

Live demo explanation:

"Super-apps are strong for payments, but shared expenses require more context. In MyPay, a collection stores the group, members, expenses, split rules, balances, and settlement state together."

### 3. Structural Limitation Of P2P And B2C Models

Research point:

Traditional payment systems model relationships as one payer to one payee. Group finance is many-to-many: the same person can be creditor in one expense and debtor in another.

MyPay relation:

The Collection module models a group as a persistent financial context. Each collection can contain many members and many expenses. Each expense produces multiple shares.

Live demo explanation:

"This collection is not just a list of transfers. It represents a many-to-many financial relationship. One person may pay for dinner, another may pay for transport, and MyPay keeps the shared state across all expenses."

### 4. Social Finance And Shared Consumption

Research point:

Social finance concerns small trusted groups such as students, housemates, families, and travel groups. These arrangements are often informal and depend on memory, trust, and social pressure.

MyPay relation:

MyPay formalizes informal shared spending into digital records: collections, expenses, shares, settlements, reports, and notifications.

Live demo explanation:

"The goal is to reduce informal memory-based debt. When I add an expense, the system records who paid, who participated, how the amount was split, and whether each share is settled."

### 5. Psychological Barriers In Debt Recovery

Research point:

People may avoid asking friends for repayment because it feels awkward or petty. Ambiguity about exact debt also increases avoidance.

MyPay relation:

MyPay acts as a neutral mediator. It calculates the amount, records it, shows balances, and creates notifications.

Live demo explanation:

"Instead of one user personally reminding another user, MyPay makes the obligation objective. The amount is generated by the system, so repayment becomes less socially uncomfortable."

### 6. Fair Division And Split Strategy Taxonomy

Research point:

Real shared expenses are not always equal. Research discusses equal, percentage, fixed/exact, hybrid, and hierarchical approaches.

MyPay relation:

MyPay implements a split strategy engine:

- Equal split.
- Percentage split.
- Exact split.
- Hybrid split.
- Hierarchical split.

Live demo explanation:

"During expense creation, I can choose a split strategy. This is implemented using the Strategy pattern. Each split type is a separate class, so the system can support different fairness models without turning the service into one large conditional block."

Recommended demo action:

Do not demo only equal split. Use percentage, hybrid, or hierarchical split because it better proves the research contribution.

### 7. Numerical Precision And Remainder Allocation

Research point:

Financial systems cannot safely use floating-point arithmetic because decimal money values can produce precision drift. Rounding creates remainder problems when amounts do not divide evenly.

MyPay relation:

MyPay uses `BigDecimal`, `MoneyUtil`, and `RemainderDistributor`.

Live demo explanation:

"Money calculation is handled with `BigDecimal`, not `double`, because financial values require decimal precision. When a total does not divide perfectly, the remainder distributor adjusts the shares so the final sum still matches the original expense amount."

Good technical point:

"This is small in the UI, but important in the backend. Even one cent of mismatch can break user trust."

### 8. Tax And Overhead Distribution

Research point:

Tax and service charges should be distributed carefully. A naive global split may cause unfairness if users consumed different amounts.

MyPay relation:

MyPay supports tax fields in expense creation and a `TaxCalculator` in the split engine.

Live demo explanation:

"This part connects directly to the research on fair division. I can record tax or service charge so the calculated share is closer to real bill behaviour."

### 9. Transaction Netting

Research point:

Gross settlement creates too many small payments. Bilateral netting reduces reciprocal debts into one final amount.

MyPay relation:

MyPay includes net settlement through `NettingSagaOrchestrator`.

Live demo explanation:

"Instead of settling every small debt individually, MyPay can calculate the net outstanding amount between two users. This reduces transaction friction and improves settlement efficiency."

Recommended demo action:

Show the `Settle Net` page after showing multiple expenses or report position.

### 10. Multi-Currency Systems

Research point:

Modern social finance may involve cross-border users, travel, and currencies. Currency conversion needs rate storage, freshness, and consistency.

MyPay relation:

MyPay supports MYR, SGD, and USD wallets, exchange rates, conversion preview, Redis caching, database fallback, and hardcoded fallback rates for prototype resilience.

Live demo explanation:

"MyPay separates the expense currency from the payment currency. If the user settles using another currency, the currency service provides conversion. Redis is used so repeated rate lookups are faster."

Important limitation to mention:

"This is a prototype using stored/configurable rates. A production version would need regulated FX integration, stronger audit logging, and rate-locking rules."

### 11. Similar Systems Research

The research compares Splitwise, Touch 'n Go eWallet, and Revolut.

#### Splitwise

Research point:

Splitwise is strong at collaborative ledgers and debt simplification, but it has a settlement gap because actual payment happens outside the app.

MyPay relation:

MyPay keeps the collection-based expense model, but connects it to wallet settlement.

Live demo explanation:

"Compared with a ledger-only app, MyPay does not stop at calculating debt. When I settle, the transaction service coordinates wallet debit, wallet credit, settlement record, and share status update."

#### Touch 'n Go eWallet

Research point:

Touch 'n Go is strong at payment convenience and domestic network effects, but splitting logic is more limited and payment is mainly immediate/pre-funded.

MyPay relation:

MyPay supports a persistent debt state before settlement, so users can record obligations first and settle later.

Live demo explanation:

"Compared with a normal e-wallet, MyPay can remember debt before payment. This is useful when users are not ready to settle immediately but still need a reliable record."

#### Revolut

Research point:

Revolut is strong at multi-currency wallets and currency agility, but collaborative features are more closed-loop and regulated.

MyPay relation:

MyPay adopts a simplified multi-currency wallet concept for academic scope while focusing more on collection-based group finance.

Live demo explanation:

"Compared with a neobank model, MyPay is not trying to implement full banking compliance. The prototype focuses on demonstrating multi-currency settlement logic within shared expense workflows."

### 12. FinTech Trilemma

Research point:

The document describes a trilemma: Logic, Liquidity, and Accessibility. Existing systems often optimize only two.

MyPay relation:

MyPay attempts to combine:

- Logic: collections and flexible split algorithms.
- Liquidity: wallet-based settlement.
- Accessibility: mobile web interface and simple onboarding for prototype users.

Live demo explanation:

"MyPay is designed to bridge the gap between calculation logic and actual settlement. The collection gives logic, the wallet gives liquidity, and the mobile-first UI supports accessibility."

### 13. Distributed Microservice Architecture

Research point:

Microservices improve modularity, maintainability, and fault isolation, but they introduce communication and consistency complexity.

MyPay relation:

MyPay separates backend responsibilities into auth, wallet, collection, transaction, currency, notification, reporting, gateway, config, and discovery services.

Live demo explanation:

"The architecture reflects domain separation. Authentication is not mixed with wallet logic, wallet logic is not mixed with expense splitting, and reporting is separated as a read-side aggregation service."

Good examiner-facing answer:

"A monolith may be simpler for a small project, but microservices let this FYP demonstrate service boundaries, distributed communication, and failure-handling patterns."

### 14. Service Communication Patterns

Research point:

Distributed systems need synchronous and asynchronous communication.

MyPay relation:

MyPay uses:

- OpenFeign for immediate service calls.
- RabbitMQ for background events.

Live demo explanation:

"During settlement, transaction service needs an immediate answer from wallet, collection, and currency services, so it uses Feign calls. For notifications, the system can publish an event and let notification service process it asynchronously."

Short sentence to memorize:

"Feign is used when the answer is needed now; RabbitMQ is used when another service can react later."

### 15. Saga-Orchestrated Settlement

Research point:

A distributed financial workflow cannot rely on a single database transaction across many services.

MyPay relation:

MyPay uses saga-style orchestration for settlement and netting.

Live demo explanation:

"When I press confirm settlement, one button triggers multiple backend steps: validate share, convert currency if needed, debit payer, credit payee, record transaction, record settlement, mark share settled, and publish notification. If a later step fails, compensation reverses completed wallet operations where possible."

This is one of the most important technical explanations in the live demo.

### 16. Security Considerations

Research point:

E-wallet systems need authentication, authorization, password protection, token management, internal endpoint protection, and role-based access control.

MyPay relation:

MyPay implements:

- JWT access and refresh tokens.
- Gateway-level JWT validation.
- Password hashing.
- Revoked refresh token table.
- Internal endpoint blocking.
- Collection role checking.
- Request/trace IDs.

Live demo explanation:

"The frontend does not call internal debit or credit endpoints directly. It goes through the gateway and transaction service so settlement is validated and orchestrated properly."

## Extra Relevant Knowledge To Mention

### ACID vs Saga

MySQL provides ACID guarantees within a single service database. However, settlement touches multiple services, so one database transaction cannot cover the whole workflow. That is why saga orchestration is used.

### Database-Per-Service Trade-Off

Each service owns its schema. This improves modularity but makes cross-service joins unavailable. MyPay handles cross-service relationships using IDs and service calls.

### Prototype vs Production

The current system is an academic prototype. Production fintech would require:

- Real payment gateway integration.
- KYC and AML compliance.
- Strong audit logging.
- Fraud detection.
- Encryption hardening.
- Monitoring and alerting.
- Load testing.
- Disaster recovery.
- Regulatory approval.

Mentioning this honestly makes the project sound more mature, not weaker.

### Why Mobile-First UI Matters

Shared expense settlement often happens at the moment of social activity: restaurant, trip, house bills, or event. A mobile-first UI fits that context better than a desktop-first system.

### Why React Query Matters

React Query helps the UI handle asynchronous server state, loading states, stale data, and refresh after mutations. For example, after settlement succeeds, wallet, collection balance, and report queries can be invalidated and refetched.
